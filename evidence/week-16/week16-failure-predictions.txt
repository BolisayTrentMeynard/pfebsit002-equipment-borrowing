      H2 (my pick, about 60%): on Windows a refused TCP connection to 127.0.0.1 takes about 2 s to be reported (several SYN tries). Each API call that needs the database then takes about 2 s (the connect attempt) plus about 0.6 s fixed cost = 2.5 to 3.5 s, and it ends as a 500.
      H1 (about 40%): the refusal is immediate. A failing call takes only the fixed cost, 0.6 to 0.9 s, FASTER than a normal call (0.85 s) because no query runs.
      Under H2 the single-threaded server is saturated: capacity is about 1 / 2.6 s = 0.38 requests per second against an offered load of about 1 per second. With 5 users (about 1.9 requests per pass, 10 s think time) and 1 monitor user, a closed-loop estimate gives an average answer time of about 10 s (range 7 to 13 s) and a throughput of about 0.4 answers per second, so only about 35 to 45 answers arrive in the 100 s "during" phase (about 30 to 38 of them user requests, 3 to 4 polls of each monitor endpoint), instead of about 100 in a normal phase.
      Under H2 /up and readiness are ALSO slow during the outage (they queue behind the failing calls): median 5 to 12 s, although /up still answers 200. "Liveness stays 200" is true; "liveness stays fast" is not. Under H1 they stay at 0.6 to 1.0 s and about 100 answers arrive.
